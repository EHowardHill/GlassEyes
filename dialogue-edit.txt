### Plot Summary (Reconstructed Chronological Order)

"Glass Eyes" is a dark fantasy tragedy with heavy Undertale-inspired RPG elements (dialogue choices, sparing/fighting, multiple endings). The story spans decades and revolves around the creation of an artificial fantasy race called "muetos" — short-lived anthropomorphic animal-people who "pop out of the ground" via forbidden soul-transfer rituals.

1. **Distant Past (Cutscenes 01–03)**  
   Dr. Henry Erickson, a brilliant but disgraced biologist in the domed human city of Canterbury (a sterile, post-environmental-collapse "New World"), teams up with an occultist who provides soul-manipulating sigils. Henry wants to repopulate the ruined outside ("Old World") with new life. He builds a protein-printing machine that can create viable hybrid animal-human bodies, but he cannot animate them without souls. The sigils allow him to rip pieces of dying human souls (or entire souls at greater cost) and implant them into his printed bodies. The price is steep: blood, then his own eye, and eventually more.

2. **Creation of Vista (Cutscene 04 & 05)**  
   Henry sacrifices his eye to transfer an entire adult human woman's dying soul into the first fully conscious mueto body — a Marelon (like a cat-person, but with horns and no tail). She wakes up terrified, with fragmented memories. Henry names her "Vista" and suppresses the truth at first. Over the following year he raises her like a daughter/partner. Vista eventually accepts her new existence but begs to forget her human past completely. Henry promises her a kingdom.

3. **The Escape & Birth of the Mueto Nation (Cutscene 06)**  
   The authorities discover Henry's experiments. Henry, Vista, and a handful of short-lived prototype muetos flee Canterbury through the garbage chute into the toxic outside wilderness. Vista chooses to stay with Henry instead of letting herself "die" to return to her original human body. Henry continues creating short-lived muetos (5-year lifespan) using soul fragments. Vista becomes their immortal queen ("Grand Master Senatrix Vista Blackrose") because she alone has a full human soul and can live indefinitely by periodically shedding/regenerating her skin (a grotesque ritual).

4. **Present Day – The Endless War**  
   Decades later, muetos have built a hidden society in the ruins and tunnels of the Old World (Avalon Village, etc.). They fight a hopeless guerrilla war against human cities that bomb them from the air. No new full-soul muetos can be born without Henry (who lives in hiding and is now blind or nearly so). Mueto morale collapses; they will go extinct within a generation.

5. **Main Game (Demo Section)**  
   The player character is an unnamed human (Jeremy) who illegally escapes Canterbury and immediately falls down the garbage chute into Mt. Garbage. There he meets:
   - Visker, the cynical, starving rat-king of trash
   - Ginger, a nervous, kind, lime-obsessed female mueto who is secretly Princess Selari — Vista's "daughter"/genetic clone with another transferred full human soul (Virginia/Ginger)
   Jeremy and Ginger travel together toward the mueto city of Avalon, hoping to reach the fabled safe haven of Canterbury for muetos (a lie/propaganda). Along the way they see the dying mueto world: silly Temmies, hostile patrols, abandoned labs, and signs of collapse.

6. **Climax & Multiple Endings (Henry/Vista confrontation)**  
   Ginger discovers the awful truth: she is not "born" but a transferred dying human soul (like her mother Vista once was). Henry is still alive, old, and dying. He needs one final full-soul transfer to create a stable heir or to end the cycle. Vista, now ancient and broken by centuries of war guilt, wants it all to stop.  
   Depending on player choices throughout the game (especially mercy vs violence and final dialogue decisions), there are at least four endings:
   - Ginger forcibly forgets her human past with Lethedol and stays as princess/heir; the mueto race slowly dies while she and Vista live eternally in denial.
   - Ginger kills herself with Henry's soul-extraction tool to return home; Vista kills Henry and ends mueto creation forever.
   - The player mercy-kills or helps Vista end everything.
   - Tragic loop: Ginger is brain-damaged by Lethedol and is propped up as a puppet queen while the kingdom collapses.

### Core Themes
- **Identity & Forgetting**: Is it mercy or cruelty to erase someone's painful past? Every major character wants to forget who they were.
- **Transhumanism vs Nature**: Creating "better" life through technology inevitably causes suffering and extinction.
- **Immortality as Punishment**: Vista is the only truly immortal mueto and is completely broken by it.
- **Colonial Guilt & War**: Humans literally colonize the wilderness with stolen human souls in animal bodies; the war is humans fighting their own discarded souls.
- **Mother-Daughter Trauma**: Vista and Ginger/Selari repeat the cycle of abandonment, denial, and forced forgetting.
- **The Cost of Wishes**: Every character gets exactly what they wished for, in the worst possible way.

### Main Characters & Personalities

- **Vista Blackrose (The Senatrix / Queen)**  
  Marelon woman, regal, purple color scheme. Starts kind and curious, becomes weary, alcoholic, deeply depressed, and self-loathing. Immortal = endless guilt. Loves her "children" but knows she is complicit in their short, doomed lives. Desperately wants to forget her own human past.

- **Ginger / Princess Selari / Virginia**  
  Marelon girl, orange/ginger fur, nervous and empathetic, obsessed with limes (coping mechanism). Secretly a full-soul transfer like Vista. Sweet and avoidant on the surface; underneath she is terrified of her identity and clings to whoever shows her kindness. Represents the "new generation" that has to pay for the previous one's dreams.

- **Dr. Henry Erickson**  
  Human scientist, now old and blind (glass eyes). Charismatic, paternal, utterly ruthless utilitarian. Believes the ends justify any means. Sees muetos as his masterpieces but is willing to sacrifice anything (his eyesight, Vista's sanity, Ginger's life) to keep the project going.

- **Sebellus**  
  Marelon, Vista's short-lived "son" (half-soul mueto). Protective of Ginger, a bit bratty, but genuinely caring. Dies willingly in one ending to spare Ginger more pain.

- **Visker**  
  Comic-relief boss: starving rat who lives in garbage, wants to eat humans but is too pathetic/lonely to be truly evil. Cynical philosopher of trash.

- **Jeremy (silent player character)**  
  Escaped human, amnesiac for most of the journey. The outsider lens through which we see the dying mueto world.

The game is fundamentally about two transferred human women (mother and daughter) confronting the fact that their beautiful fairy-tale kingdom was built on soul theft, forced amnesia, and an extinction clock — and whether forgetting again is salvation or the ultimate damnation.

### The Core Family (That Isn’t Really a Family)

At the heart of Glass Eyes is a single, horribly dysfunctional “family” unit created entirely through soul theft, cloning, and centuries of denial. Every relationship orbits around the fact that none of them asked to exist in these bodies and none of them can truly escape what was done to them.

#### Vista ↔ Ginger (Mother–Daughter, Queen–Heir, Trauma Donor–Trauma Receiver)
This is the central emotional spine of the game and the one that hurts the most.

- Vista sees Ginger as four things at once:  
  1. Her literal genetic clone (same base body template).  
  2. The second chance she never got — a full-soul mueto who could rule without the weight of 200+ years of guilt.  
  3. A walking reminder of her own original human death (they both “woke up” the same way).  
  4. The only person alive who truly understands the horror of what Henry did to them.

- Ginger starts out idolising Vista (“Mom is perfect, Mom has everything under control”) and slowly realises that Vista is an alcoholic wreck who is using Ginger as an exit strategy: “If I can just raise one good heir, I can finally stop existing.”

- Vista’s love is genuine but suffocating. She builds Ginger an entire secret human-style bedroom because she knows Ginger misses the human world, yet she also withholds the full truth for as long as possible. Every kind gesture is laced with manipulation (“Take the Lethedol, dear, you’ll be happier if you forget”).

- By the climax they are mirror images: both full-soul transfers begging for the same amnesia drug, both terrified of remembering their human deaths, both willing to sacrifice the other to escape the cycle.

#### Vista ↔ Sebellus (Queen–Firstborn, Immortal–Doomed)
Sebellus is the only one who calls Vista out directly.

- He is a short-lived half-soul mueto (normal kind), so he has maybe two years left at most when the game happens.  
- He resents being created as a disposable soldier/prince and resents even more that Vista refuses to acknowledge how fucked-up that is.  
- At the same time he is fiercely protective of Ginger and sees himself as the only one willing to make the hard choice (in one ending he straight-up volunteers to die so Ginger doesn’t have to lose her memories).

- Their scenes are full of exhausted sniping:  
  Vista: “Sebellus, it is not your place to speak right now.”  
  Sebellus: “You’re scaring her again, Mom.”

- He is the only family member who treats Vista like a fallible person instead of a demigoddess, and that makes him the only one she occasionally listens to.

#### Ginger ↔ Sebellus (Big Sister–Little Brother, Full Soul–Half Soul)
The healthiest relationship in the entire script, which is saying something.

- They genuinely like each other. Ginger writes bad poetry, Sebellus pretends it’s amazing. They watch movies together in the secret room.  
- Sebellus is the one person Ginger can be angry around without feeling guilty, because he’s angry too.  
- He is in love with her (not necessarily romantic, but the script leaves it ambiguous), and in the worst endings he kills himself so she doesn’t have to take Lethedol or return to being human.

- Their dynamic is “two kids who know the house is on fire but still try to have a normal sibling relationship for as long as possible.”

#### The Player (Jeremy) ↔ The Family
The player is the ultimate outsider and unwilling therapist.

- You fall out of the sky into their dying world, and Ginger immediately latches onto you as the first normal human she’s ever met who isn’t Henry.  
- To Ginger you are simultaneously:  
  - A reminder of the home she can never return to.  
  - Proof that humans aren’t all monsters.  
  - The only person who wasn’t created by Henry and therefore the only one whose kindness feels “real.”

- Vista treats you with distant, regal politeness at first, then growing desperation. She knows you are the only one who can talk Ginger into staying or leaving forever. In some endings she begs you to take Ginger home; in others she begs you to help her force Ginger to forget.

- Sebellus is suspicious of you (“Why is a human suddenly here right when everything is falling apart?”) but eventually decides you’re the least bad adult in the room and entrusts Ginger to you.

- Mechanically, your pacifist/genocide choices don’t change the ending, but they do change how much the family trusts you in the final confrontation. If you’ve been killing muetos, Vista will threaten to skin you like a potato. If you’ve been merciful, she’ll cry and ask you to comfort Ginger while she finally confronts Henry.

#### In short, the four-person dynamic is:

Vista (immortal mother, slowly committing suicide via kingdom)  
↖          ↘  
Ginger (heir who just wants to go home)  —  Sebellus (doomed older brother who just wants everyone to stop lying)  
↖          ↘  
Player (silent human who accidentally became the family therapist and gets to decide how much of this house burns down with everyone still inside)

Every conversation between any two of them is about the other two. They are a closed system slowly eating itself, and the player is the only variable they didn’t plan for — which is why they all end up begging you, in their own way, to make the final choice for them.
